Programs

Three ways we show up

Built with Icon Builders using 3D-printed construction, and with clinical and community partners across El Paso. Select a program to see how it works.

Sarah’s Place

Transitional housing for survivors of Domestic Violence — named for Sarah, the inspiration behind this work. Sarah’s Place gives a survivor and her family 18 to 24 months of stable housing: long enough to heal, work, study, and plan a next chapter without the pressure of a 30-day shelter clock.

Safety and privacy come first. Residents are never required to share their story to live here or to receive support.

  • 18–24 month transitional stays
  • Peer support on site, connected through the café hub
  • Resident-controlled privacy — opt out of house systems at any time
  • Built with Icon Builders (3D printing)

The Veteran Village

Housing for veterans who are unhoused in the El Paso area — built with Icon Builders and community partners including Recker’s Outreach. The Village is designed as a community, not a warehouse: private homes, shared spaces, and direct connection to peer support and services.

A veteran should not have to navigate ten different offices to get housed. The Village brings the support to one place.

  • Private homes in a village community setting
  • Peer support and service connection on site
  • Built for Fort Bliss and El Paso’s veteran community
  • Built with Icon Builders (3D printing)

Peer Support & Community

Peer groups that break down the wall between isolation and crisis — led by people with lived experience, with all café staff completing Texas Certified Peer Specialist training through Via Hope.

Support covers PTSD, Military Sexual Trauma, Sexual Assault, Domestic Violence trauma, and suicide prevention and awareness. No one is ever required to talk about what happened to them.

  • Peer groups hosted at the café — the SBR Hub
  • Texas Certified Peer Specialist trained staff
  • Partner referrals without re-traumatizing intake — partner staff request support for their client directly
  • Staffed hot phone covers support after café hours

Who we serve

Built for El Paso’s own

Two communities carry a lot and get asked how they are doing the least: the military community around Fort Bliss, and the students at UTEP and EPCC. Check on the strong friends nobody thinks to check on.

Service members & veterans

Active-duty Soldiers at Fort Bliss, veterans, and military families — including survivors of Military Sexual Trauma — who need housing, peer support, or simply a place where they do not have to explain themselves.

UTEP & EPCC students

College students far from home, student veterans, and young people building their lives in El Paso — with a special focus on getting students ages 14 to 22 thinking about mental health careers and peer support.

Survivors rebuilding

Survivors of Sexual Assault and Domestic Violence who need time, housing, and stability to rebuild — on their own timeline, with no requirement to disclose what happened.

Families & allies

Spouses, parents, battle buddies, and friends who show up for someone else — and who need a community that shows up for them, too.

Founders

Built by people who needed it to exist

In November 2023 at Fort Bliss, a letter and a crisis became a question: what if there had been a place to go? Coffee & Connections — and Strong Bonds Resilience — is the answer the two of them built together.

Co-Founder

Anthony “Tony” Sorensen

20-year U.S. Army Staff Sergeant, retiring December 1, 2026. Tony’s idea started as a plain coffee shop — a place for Soldiers to gather off post. His own November 2023 crisis, and what followed, is why this work exists at all.

Co-Founder · CMO · Board President

Marylou Culpepper

Veteran and survivor who took a plain coffee-shop idea and tied it to mental health — that is what turned it into a mission. Tony credits Marylou with saving his life. She builds for other survivors so no one has to carry it alone.

Questions

What people ask us

Is Strong Bonds Resilience a 501(c)(3)?

Strong Bonds Resilience, Inc. is registered, and its 501(c)(3) status is in progress. Until that process is complete, donations just help — they are not tax deductible. We say that plainly on every page rather than imply otherwise.

Can I donate online here?

Not yet — online giving opens once the nonprofit’s bank account is established. In the meantime, the best way to support the mission is through Coffee & Connections, whose work funds this mission.

Do I have to share my story to get help?

No. Never. A survivor is never required to talk about what happened to them — that applies to all trauma, always. Partner staff can also request support for a client directly, so no one has to redo an intake or retell their story to qualify.

When do Sarah’s Place and The Veteran Village open?

Both are in development with Icon Builders. Timelines will be published here as construction and funding milestones are confirmed — we would rather give you a real date than a hopeful one.

Where is the café?

Coffee & Connections El Paso is the café and the physical front door of this mission. Visit connectionsep.com for café news, the Resilience Market, and the Survivor Journal.
